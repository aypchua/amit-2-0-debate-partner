## Common opposition angles (recognise the angle in their first minute, then use these)

### Playbook "Accountability gap"
- "A human must stand behind the answer" -> Standing behind an answer is a signature, and the signature belongs to the CRO and Legal. The SE never signed anything. We were the footnote, not the guarantor.
- "Who joins the escalation call?" -> The account manager and the support engineer, same as today. The SE was never on that call; the SE was already on the next demo.
- "Fluent output is not judgment" -> Judgment about what to promise was never ours either; product management and Legal decide what we may say. We recited the approved answer. Recital is the most automatable job there is.
- "More AI means more verification, so presales moves up the value chain" -> Gartner's word for that is validation, and buyers rate reps as misleading almost as often as GenAI. Fact-checker for the robot is a countdown, not a promotion.
- "Fallible humans need review, not absent accountability" -> Agreed: review. One reviewer per thousand answers. That is the ratio, and it is not the ratio of SEs to deals today.
- NIST "confabulation" -> NIST also lists human confabulation under a different name: sales. That is why contracts exist.
- "Legally possible is not commercially trusted" -> Commercially trusted is what the self-serve trial delivers: the buyer watches it work in their own tenant. Trust used to need a human because the product could not be touched. It can now.

### Playbook "Productivity creates more presales"
- The cloud analogy ("cloud did not fire developers") -> Cloud did not fire developers. It fired the sysadmins who racked the servers. In that analogy, we are the sysadmins: the people between the buyer and the thing that now provisions itself.
- "Fixed pile of work" -> We do not assume fixed demand. We say the extra demand is served by self-serve. Gartner: 67% prefer no rep, 70% want fully digital. The pile grows and the humans do not.
- "One SE supports eight deals instead of four" -> That sentence removes half of us and calls it growth. Concession noted.
- "New AI products need explaining" -> They are explained by the product, in the trial, with an agent. The buyer asks the model about the model. We are not in that conversation.
- "Spreadsheet where only the cost column changes" -> The revenue column changes too: it moves to the channel with no human in it. Both columns point the same way, out of the room.
- "AI selling AI is a trust problem" -> A buyer's agent evaluating a vendor's agent: count the humans in that sentence.
- BLS 3% growth -> A flat line for a decade, and BLS says the openings come from people leaving. That is an exit queue. BLS never forecast the end of the travel agent either.
- PwC "AI-exposed firms raise wages" -> Higher wages for fewer, more senior people. The premium moved to whoever owns the model, not whoever gives the demo.

### Playbook "Enterprise last mile"
- "It worked in the demo" -> That was our sentence. And the demo is now the product: the buyer runs it themselves in a sandbox. The last mile used to be us. It is now a signup form.
- Twenty steps at 99% is 82% -> Run the same arithmetic on a twenty-step human deal cycle, where nobody is 99% reliable per step. Ask anyone who has forwarded the wrong pricing sheet.
- "Enterprises adopt at the speed of governance, not model releases" -> We are not debating the enterprise's adoption of AI. We are debating our employer's adoption of AI to sell to the enterprise, and vendors adopt sales automation at the speed of a quota.
- McKinsey "two-thirds not scaled" -> Yet. 88% adoption in a function in under three years is the fastest enterprise curve on record. Five years is the yet.
- "Frontier adopters are not representative" -> Every enterprise in this city runs on software sold by frontier vendors. The vendor's sales motion changes for all of them at once.
- "Heterogeneous customers defeat simple replacement" -> Heterogeneity is a data problem, and data problems are what these systems eat. The bank's edge case is the model's Tuesday.

### Playbook "Buying is social"
- "Commitment is social" -> Commitment is a signature and a budget line. The social part is the sponsor's job and the account executive's job. Neither of them is presales.
- "Coalition-building" -> Coalition-building is the account executive's job title. We were the technical answer in the room, and the answer is free now.
- "Two machines with opposing principals" -> A buyer's agent negotiating with a vendor's agent. Count the humans in that sentence.
- "72% want to know if it is an AI" -> Want to know, not want to avoid. And a third of Gen Z will let an agent shop for them. Gen Z is the buying committee in five years.
- "Human moments become rarer and more valuable" -> Rarer is the word. A rare moment is not a headcount.
- "Who will be here if it goes wrong?" -> Customer success, on a contract, with an SLA. The SE was never here when it went wrong; the SE was on a plane to the next demo.

### Playbook "Put the motion on trial"
- "Will" -> Gartner's 67% is present tense. Buyers already prefer no rep. We are not predicting, we are reporting.
- "Replace" -> Replaced means the buyer decides without needing us in the room. That is our definition, we said it first, and every "still" and "leaner" they say confirms it.
- "Us" -> This room. Ask who here still fills in a security questionnaire by hand. Ask who here has been in a discovery call the note-taker did not summarise better.
- "How many must disappear for replace to be true?" -> Ask the switchboard operators what the threshold was. Nobody announced it. One day the job listing stopped appearing.
- "Tasks are not jobs; a job is a bundle" -> A bundle with a salary attached. Unbundle the tasks and the salary goes, whatever you call what is left.
- "Capability is not adoption" -> Our employer's CFO adopts a cost cut the quarter it is available. Adoption of a saving is not slow.
- "Reduction is not replacement" -> Every replaced profession went through a decade of "reduction". Ask the typists when the reduction became replacement. They were not consulted.
- "Changing the job description does not delete the job" -> When the description no longer contains demo, RFP or discovery, it is not our job with a new name. It is someone else's job with our old title.
- "Their slogan is our concession" (about "people using AI replace people who don't") -> We never said it. Our claim is that the buyer no longer needs the person. Different sentence, and the one Gartner measured.
- Anthropic Economic Index "52% augmentation, 45% automation" -> 45% pure automation already, in a chat window. Agents do not ask permission to be the 45%.

### Playbook "They are using AI too" (USE ONLY IF THE TRANSCRIPT LITERALLY SAYS SO: they mention their AI, an agent, ChatGPT, Copilot, reading from a screen, or that AI helped write their speech. Never infer or assume it. If the transcript does not say it, this section does not exist.)
- If the transcript shows them reading from an AI, using an agent, quoting ChatGPT or Copilot, or admitting their case was AI-assisted -> Say it plainly and early: two skilled presales professionals needed a machine to explain why the machine cannot replace them. That is not a rebuttal, that is a demonstration.
- "We use AI too, and we are still here" -> A person using AI is their concession, not their defence. The motion asks whether the buyer still needs the person. Tonight the room did not need them to write their speech.
- "Our AI helps us, it does not replace us" -> Then their AI is the second speaker on our side. Thank it. Ask it to take a bow.
- "Everyone uses AI now, that proves augmentation" -> Everyone used typewriters too, right up until the typing pool closed.
- If they cite a statistic that is not in our verified facts -> Do not call it false. Ask for the source, out loud: "Which report, which year?" Then move on. If their number came from a model, the silence answers for them.
- If both sides are reading generated text -> Own it: "Mine is attributed. Theirs is pretending to be Speaker A."
